The message you keep meaning to send

It is 9 p.m. The family WhatsApp group has 214 unread messages. A forwarded prayer, a wedding contribution list, a video of a goat that escaped. You scroll to the bottom and put the phone down.

Strange. You were around people all day, and you feel like nobody asked how you are.

You are not being dramatic. That feeling is real, and your brain takes it seriously.

What your brain is doing

Humans evolved in groups. For most of our history, being cut off from the group was dangerous, so many researchers think the brain keeps watch on belonging much as it watches hunger. When belonging feels thin, you feel it.

Here is how much it matters. In 2010, a team led by Julianne Holt-Lunstad combined 148 studies covering 308,849 people. Those with stronger social relationships were 50% more likely to survive over the follow-up period. The authors said the size of the effect was comparable to quitting smoking.

This is a link, not proof that friendship alone caused it. But it is a strong, consistent link, and it tells us connection is not a luxury. It is part of how a body and mind stay well.

Try it now: two minutes

Do this before you read on.

  1. Pick one person who has been kind to you. It can be someone you have not spoken to in years.
  2. Send one line. Short is fine: Thinking of you. How are you? Or be direct: Rough day. Do you have five minutes to talk tonight?
  3. Put the phone down. You do not need a reply to have done the hard part.

The first line is the hardest. You just wrote it.

Make it stick

Pick a cue you already have, and attach the message to it:

If–then planIf it is Sunday evening, then I send one message to one person I trust.

A fixed cue beats a good intention. Intentions fade. Cues keep showing up.

When it is more than this

If the lonely feeling has lasted weeks, comes with hopelessness, or you cannot think of one person to message, that is a good moment to talk to a licensed psychologist. You do not have to be in crisis to deserve support.

If you are in danger or thinking of ending your life, call 999 or 112 now, or open the Emergency page.

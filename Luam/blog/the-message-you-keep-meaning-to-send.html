A little more self-awareness can change a conversation

You know what you meant to say. The other person only knows how it sounded.

Perhaps you were tired and answered a message with one word. Perhaps you were following up with a client, speaking to a colleague, discussing plans at home or asking someone for help—and your words landed differently from how you intended. Later, someone says, gently, “You’ve seemed a little distant lately.” It catches you by surprise. From your side, the day had felt ordinary.

This small gap between intention and impact is part of being human. You experience your thoughts from the inside; other people meet your words and actions from the outside. Neither view is the whole picture. Both can tell you something useful.

The space between what you mean and what lands

Organizational psychologist Tasha Eurich describes two sides of self-awareness: understanding your own inner world, and understanding how other people experience you. Being familiar with one does not automatically make the other clear.

In her research, only 10–15% of the people studied met the criteria for strong self-awareness. That figure comes from the researchers’ own work, published in Harvard Business Review, so it is best treated as an estimate rather than a universal headcount. The more useful point is modest: we all have blind spots, and a person who knows us well may help us notice one.

Feedback does not have to become a verdict. It can simply be another angle on an interaction you were part of.

Try a question with a little more room in it

Eurich recommends asking “what” questions rather than getting stuck on “why”. “Why am I like this?” can send you around the same familiar loop. “What was happening just before I became short with them?” gives you a moment you can look at more closely.

A small conversation · when it feels right

Think of someone who knows you well and can be honest without being unkind. There is no need to make this a serious meeting.

  1. Choose a calm moment when neither of you is rushing.
  2. Ask: When I’m under pressure, what do you tend to notice about me?
  3. Let them finish. You can ask for an example, then take a little time before responding.

You do not have to agree with every observation. A useful piece of feedback is something to consider, not a court ruling.

Give yourself a moment before replying

If a comment stings, you can pause without withdrawing from the conversation. A slow breath, a sip of water or a moment to look out of the window can give you time to hear what was said.

If–then planIf someone I trust says something about me that stings, then I’ll pause and ask, “What did you notice?” before I explain my side.

Keep what is useful

Some feedback will fit; some will not. Look for a specific example rather than trying to decide whether you are a good or bad person. You might notice that you go quiet when overwhelmed, or that your attempts to solve a problem can sound like you are dismissing someone’s feelings.

That is information you can work with. It does not erase your intentions, and it does not make the other person’s experience less real.

When a conversation needs support

If the same conflicts keep returning, or it feels difficult to talk without fear, blame or shutting down, a licensed psychologist can help you understand the pattern and practise safer ways to communicate.

If you are in immediate danger or thinking of ending your life, call 999 or 112 in Kenya, or open the Emergency page.

Kujielewa zaidi kunaweza kubadilisha mazungumzo

Wewe unajua ulichokusudia kusema. Mtu mwingine anajua tu jinsi kilivyosikika.

Labda ulikuwa umechoka na ukajibu ujumbe kwa neno moja. Labda ulikuwa unamfuatilia mteja, unazungumza na mwenzako kazini, unapanga mambo nyumbani au unaomba msaada—lakini maneno yako yakafika tofauti na ulivyokusudia. Baadaye mtu anakuambia kwa upole, “Siku hizi unaonekana uko mbali kidogo.” Unashangaa. Kwako, siku ilikuwa ya kawaida tu.

Pengo hili dogo kati ya nia na athari ni sehemu ya maisha ya watu. Wewe hupitia mawazo yako ukiwa ndani yake; wengine hukutana na maneno na matendo yako kutoka nje. Hakuna upande unaoeleza picha nzima. Kila mmoja unaweza kukufundisha jambo.

Kati ya ulichomaanisha na kilichofika

Mwanasaikolojia wa mashirika Tasha Eurich anaeleza pande mbili za kujitambua: kuelewa ulimwengu wako wa ndani, na kuelewa jinsi wengine wanavyokupokea. Kuwa na ufahamu wa upande mmoja hakumaanishi kuwa mwingine uko wazi moja kwa moja.

Katika utafiti wake, ni asilimia 10–15 tu ya watu waliochunguzwa waliokidhi vigezo vya kujitambua kwa kiwango cha juu. Takwimu hiyo imetokana na kazi ya watafiti iliyochapishwa katika Harvard Business Review; kwa hiyo ichukulie kama makadirio, si hesabu inayowahusu watu wote. Jambo la msingi ni rahisi: sote tuna maeneo tusiyoyaona vizuri, na mtu anayetufahamu anaweza kutusaidia kuyaona.

Maoni ya mtu mwingine si lazima yawe hukumu. Yanaweza kuwa mtazamo mwingine kuhusu tukio mliloshiriki.

Uliza swali linaloacha nafasi ya kuelewa

Eurich anapendekeza kutumia maswali ya “nini” badala ya kukwama kwenye “kwa nini”. “Kwa nini niko hivi?” linaweza kukurudisha kwenye mzunguko uleule. “Ni nini kilikuwa kinaendelea kabla sijamjibu kwa ukali?” linakupa tukio maalumu la kulitazama.

Mazungumzo mafupi · wakati unaofaa

Fikiria mtu anayekufahamu na anayeweza kuwa mkweli bila kukudhalilisha. Huhitaji kuifanya iwe kikao rasmi.

  1. Chagua wakati mtulivu ambapo hakuna anayeharakisha.
  2. Uliza: Ninapokuwa na msongo, huwa unaona nini kwangu?
  3. Msikilize amalize. Unaweza kuomba mfano, kisha ujipatie muda kabla ya kujibu.

Huhitaji kukubaliana na kila alichosema. Maoni yenye manufaa ni jambo la kulitafakari, si hukumu ya mahakamani.

Jipe muda kabla ya kujibu

Maoni yakikuuma, unaweza kusimama kidogo bila kuondoka kwenye mazungumzo. Pumzi ya taratibu, funda la maji au kutazama nje kwa muda kunaweza kukupa nafasi ya kusikia kilichosemwa.

Mpango wa ikiwa-basiMtu ninayemwamini akisema jambo linaloniumiza kuhusu tabia yangu, basi nitasimama kidogo na kuuliza, “Umeona nini hasa?” kabla sijaeleza upande wangu.

Baki na kile chenye manufaa

Baadhi ya maoni yatakugusa; mengine hayatakufaa. Tafuta mfano maalumu badala ya kuamua kama wewe ni mtu mzuri au mbaya. Unaweza kugundua kuwa hunyamaza unapolemewa, au kuwa unapojaribu kutatua tatizo unasikika kana kwamba unapuuza hisia za mwenzako.

Hayo ni maelezo unayoweza kuyafanyia kazi. Hayafuti nia yako, wala hayafanyi uzoefu wa mwenzako usiwe wa kweli.

Wakati mazungumzo yanahitaji msaada

Ikiwa migogoro ileile inajirudia, au ni vigumu kuzungumza bila hofu, lawama au kujifungia, mwanasaikolojia aliyeidhinishwa anaweza kukusaidia kuelewa mtindo huo na kujifunza njia salama zaidi za kuwasiliana.

Ukiwa katika hatari ya haraka au ukifikiria kujidhuru, piga 999 au 112 nchini Kenya, au fungua ukurasa wa Dharura.

Psychiatrist or psychologist? Who to see first

The two roles overlap but they are not the same. A plain-language guide to who does what, and how to choose where to start.

If you have ever stared at a booking page unsure which to pick, you are not alone. Both professionals help with mental health. They train differently and offer different tools.

The short version

Psychiatrist Psychologist
Training Medical doctor, then specialist training Degree and postgraduate training in psychology
Can prescribe medication Yes No
Main tools Assessment, diagnosis, medication, some therapy Assessment, talk therapies, testing

When to start with a psychiatrist

  • You suspect a condition that may need medication, such as severe depression, bipolar disorder or psychosis.
  • Sleep, appetite or mood have changed sharply.
  • You are already taking medication and want a review.

When to start with a psychologist

  • You want to understand patterns, process grief, or build coping skills.
  • You are looking for structured therapy such as CBT.
  • Your difficulties are around stress, relationships or life transitions.

Often, it is both

Many people see a psychiatrist for diagnosis and medication and a psychologist for therapy, with the two professionals sharing notes (with your consent).

Not sure? Start anywhere

A first appointment is partly about finding the right fit. Either professional can refer you onward.

Want to talk it through?

A first conversation is about understanding what you need, not labelling you.

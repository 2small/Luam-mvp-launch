Niksen: the Dutch art of doing nothing, and why your mind may need it

Niksen means doing something with no purpose. Here is what that looks like, why it can calm an overworked mind, and how to try it without guilt.

Most of us treat rest as a reward we earn after finishing everything. Niksen (pronounced NICK-sen) is a Dutch idea that turns that around: it is the practice of doing something with no goal at all. You are not meditating, not scrolling, not "optimising your downtime". You are just letting your attention wander.

What niksen is, and what it is not

Niksen can be staring out of a window, listening to rain, or sitting with a cup of tea and no phone. It is different from mindfulness, which asks you to direct your attention. In niksen nothing is asked of you.

It is also not the same as numbing out. If you find that "doing nothing" leaves you restless, flat or tearful for days at a time, that is useful information, and worth talking about with a professional.

Why it can help

When we stop focusing on a task, the brain's default mode network becomes more active. This is the system linked to daydreaming, planning and making sense of experience. Many people notice that ideas and solutions surface when they are not trying.

  • It interrupts the stress loop. A few quiet minutes lower the pressure of constant output.
  • It makes room for creativity. Unstructured time is where connections form.
  • It rebuilds tolerance for stillness, which many of us have lost to endless notifications.

How to try it

  1. Pick ten minutes. Put your phone in another room.
  2. Sit somewhere comfortable and let your eyes rest on something ordinary.
  3. When your mind drifts, let it. When it jumps to your to-do list, notice and let that drift too.
  4. Stop when the time is up. There is nothing to score.

If it feels uncomfortable

Guilt and restlessness are common at first. Start with three minutes and build up. If stillness reliably brings up anxious or distressing thoughts, you do not have to push through alone.

When to seek support

Rest helps ordinary stress. It does not replace care for persistent low mood, panic, sleeplessness or thoughts of self-harm. If this sounds like you, a conversation with a clinician is a sensible next step.

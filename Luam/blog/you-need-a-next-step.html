Purpose 3 min read

You do not need a calling. You need a next step.

It is Monday, 6 a.m. The alarm goes off. Before your feet touch the floor, a question arrives: What am I even doing this for?

If you are carrying fees, rent, or a job search, that question can feel heavy and a little unfair. Nobody gave you a spare hour to “find your passion.”

Good news: you do not need one. Purpose is smaller and more practical than the posters suggest.

What your brain is doing

Without a clear aim, attention tends to drift toward worry. A small, specific aim gives it somewhere to go.

The research on purpose is striking. A 2019 University of Michigan study followed nearly 7,000 adults over 50 for five years. Those with the lowest purpose scores were more than twice as likely to die during that time as those with the highest. The authors could not rule out that illness lowered purpose rather than the other way round, so this shows a link, not a cure. But it tells us that having something to get up for is not soft.

Here is the practical half. A 2006 review of 94 studies found that “if-then” plans, such as “If it is 7 a.m., then I open my notes,” had a medium-to-large positive effect on whether people reached their goals. The effect size was d = 0.65. The plan does the remembering for you.

Try it now: two minutes

  1. Finish the sentence, in writing: Today counts if I ___. Keep it small and yours. I call my mother. I send one application. I write the first page. I sell my first batch before noon.
  2. Choose the first two-minute step. Open the document. Write the first line. Lay out the ingredients.
  3. Do it now, then read the last section.

This is purpose at the right size: one day, one sentence, one step.

Make it stick

Turn the step into an if-then plan:

If–then planIf I finish my morning tea, then I open the document and write one line.

If the day gets away from you, you have lost nothing. Tomorrow, write a new sentence.

When it is more than this

If nothing feels worth doing for more than two weeks, or you feel hopeless, speak to a licensed psychologist or doctor. That flat feeling can be a sign of depression, and depression is treatable.

If you are in danger or thinking of ending your life, call 999 or 112 now, or open the Emergency page.

Understanding therapy

A professional conversation with a method behind it.

It is closer to working with a coach or a doctor than to talking with a friend. The difference is training, structure and confidentiality.

What happens in a session

You talk through what is on your mind. Your clinician asks questions, points out patterns you may not see from the inside, and works with you on specific ways to respond differently.

Who you work with

A licensed psychologist, who provides talking therapy, or a psychiatrist, a medical doctor who can also assess for medication. Both are trained to tell ordinary strain from something that needs treatment.

Why you do not have to be in crisis

Most people get more from therapy when they start before things break down. You can come to understand a pattern, make a decision, or get through a change with support.

Therapy treats and explains. It is not a substitute for a wellness routine, and a wellness routine is not a substitute for treatment when you need it.

When therapy might be useful

Situations people bring to a first session.

Work follows you home

Targets, long hours and a job you cannot easily leave. You are tired in a way sleep does not fix.

Everyone is counting on you

Rent, school fees, family obligations. You handle it all, and nobody asks how you are.

The same argument, again

A partner, a parent or a colleague, and a pattern you can see but cannot stop.

A big change

A new job, a move, a loss, a new baby, a relationship ending. Life has changed and you have not caught up.

A mind that does not switch off

Worry, racing thoughts, a short temper, or feelings that arrive too strongly to manage.

Who am I outside my roles?

You are doing well on paper and still feel unsure what you want, or who you are becoming.

Finding life hard does not mean something is wrong with you, and you do not need a label to start. If you are unsure, the first session is for working that out. For specific conditions such as depression, anxiety or trauma, see what we treat.

What to expect

Four steps, and you stay in charge of each.

  1. Book

    Choose a time online, or call and we will match you with a clinician. Your details stay on your device until you send them.

  2. First consultation

    A 45 to 60 minute session to understand what you are facing. You can ask anything about how this works.

  3. Agree your goals

    You and your clinician decide what you want to work on and how, whether that is therapy, medication, both, or a referral.

  4. Ongoing sessions

    You meet regularly and review how it is going. Outcomes differ for each person, so no one can promise a timeline.

Questions

Before you book.

Do I need to have a mental health condition to attend therapy?

No. People come at very different stages: some in a hard season, some after months of feeling unlike themselves. Wanting to understand yourself better, or a pattern that keeps costing you, is a good enough reason to book.

What happens during my first session?

A 45 to 60 minute conversation. Your clinician asks what brought you in, a little of your history, and what support would look like for you. You do not need the right words. It is a conversation, not a test.

How confidential are therapy sessions?

What you discuss with your clinician stays between you, in line with professional and legal confidentiality standards. The only exceptions are situations where you or someone else is at risk, and your clinician will explain those to you clearly. The details you enter when booking stay on your device until you choose to send them. Read more on the Privacy page.

How much does therapy cost?

KES 3,000 per session, in person or by video, paid at the clinic. Sliding-scale support is available: ask us when you book. If you have medical insurance, message us and we will tell you whether it can apply.

How often should I attend?

It depends on what you are working on, so there is no fixed number. You and your clinician agree a rhythm after the first session and review it as you go.

Can I attend therapy even if I am not sure what is bothering me?

Yes. Working that out is a normal use of the first session. Describe what you notice, such as sleeping badly, short temper, or dreading Mondays, and your clinician will help you make sense of it.

Is therapy available online?

Yes. Most of our clinicians offer video sessions, and some people do their whole course of care this way. In-person sessions are in Nairobi.

How do I choose the right therapist?

Psychologists provide talking therapy. Psychiatrists are medical doctors who can also assess for medication. If you are not sure which you need, tell us what you are experiencing when you book and we will suggest a clinician.
